# Context menus

What a row can do besides open is in its menu.

**A row's menu opens with a right-click, never from a button on the row.**
A list is read down its last column: a kebab that shows under the pointer
takes the cell the state sits in, and the column changes as the pointer
passes. A file row's last cell stays what happened to the file: its conflict
mark, its reviewed tick or its letter. A sidebar row's stays its counts, and
its unread dot after them.

**The keyboard opens the same menu.** With a row selected, Shift-F10 or the
menu key opens it at the row, so nothing in it needs the pointer.

**What's done most has a home besides the menu.** A right-click menu shows
only to someone who looks for it, so an action people reach for often also
gets a key or a button where its work is, as committing and discarding have.
